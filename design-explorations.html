On the quiet mornings of August

There is a kind of silence that belongs only to August — not the silence of absence, but the silence of fullness, when the heat has absorbed every sound and the air itself seems to hold its breath. I have been waking early to catch it, sitting on the porch with coffee that cools too fast in the breeze.

I packed light for the coast: one camera, three rolls of Portra 400, and a notebook I never opened. By the end of the week I had twenty-seven frames worth keeping and a clearer sense of what I was actually looking for.

Why I still write letters

The last letter I received was from my grandmother, twelve years ago. I have kept it in a drawer beside my desk, and sometimes, when the screens become too much, I take it out and read it again.

The colour of heat at three in the afternoon

It is not orange, as I had always assumed. It is the colour of old brass — a dull, heavy gold that sits on the road and on the roofs and makes everything look like it has been there for a very long time.
